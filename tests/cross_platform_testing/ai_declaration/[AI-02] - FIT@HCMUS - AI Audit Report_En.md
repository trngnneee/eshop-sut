## 5. Conclusion — When should AI be used (or not)?

Write 80–150 words describing patterns you observed. Where did AI shine? Where did AI fail? What is your recommendation for using AI in this kind of work in the future?

AI was the only realistic way to re-execute 66 checklist items on 3 platforms (198 executions) inside the time box, and it was excellent at exactly the part humans do worst: recording the raw value behind every verdict, which is what turned "all three browsers look the same" into 29 measured divergences (1 that flips the verdict, 28 in the displayed values). It was weakest at the meta-decisions — the evidence layer it first produced could not show a URL bar at all, its overlay hid the very header some items were about, and its own run procedure would have polluted the comparison it was designed to make. Rule I take away: let AI build the instrument and read the dials, but never let it decide what counts as a valid measurement, and always ask "what would this look like if it were wrong?" before believing a verdict that flips per platform.
